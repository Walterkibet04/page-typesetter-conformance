FOLIO HOUSE STYLE - description of the layout

This document describes what the typesetter does. It is a description, not a full
specification: the file format, the output format and the page geometry are given
exactly, but the tuning of the rules (penalties, thresholds, minimum counts, tie
rules, rounding) is not. For all of that, /app/folio_ref is the authority. It is the
reference build of the typesetter: "folio_ref file.fol" prints the correct output for
any valid document (use "-" to read the document from standard input). A program is
correct when its output for a document is the same as folio_ref's.

Everything below that is marked "(given)" is exact. Where a rule is described without
numbers, find them by experiment.

1. INPUT (given)

A document is blocks separated by blank lines. A block that starts with "# " is a
level-1 heading, one that starts with "## " a level-2 heading; the rest of the block
(trimmed) is the heading text. Any other block is a paragraph: its lines are joined
with single spaces and its words are separated by white space.

A paragraph that starts with "^" is a drop-cap paragraph: the "^" is removed and the
first letter of the first word is the drop cap.
A "~" inside a word is a no-break space (U+00A0) in the output.
A hyphen inside a word is a hard hyphen, where the word may be broken.
A footnote is written [[text]] straight after the word it belongs to (no space); the
word gets a footnote marker and the footnotes are numbered 1, 2, 3, ... through the
whole document in the order they appear.

An anchor is written {#label} at the very start of a paragraph block (before any "^"),
label made of lower-case letters and digits. A reference is written {@label} inside a
word; it is replaced by the page number of the anchor with that label, as a lower-case
roman numeral, before the word is laid out (so it is part of the word, with its width).
A reference to an unknown label prints "?".

2. OUTPUT (given)

typeset(text) returns {"pages": [page, ...]}; a page is a list of entries in this
order: the body entries as they are placed, then (if the page has footnotes) one
"rule" entry, then the "fn" entries. An entry is a dictionary:
  heading   {"k": "h1" or "h2", "pg", "y", "x": 0, "t": text}
  drop cap  {"k": "cap", "pg", "y", "x": 0, "t": the letter, "r": 3}
  body line {"k": "body", "pg", "y", "x": indent, "t": text, "g": [gap widths]}
  rule      {"k": "rule", "pg", "y", "x": 0}
  footnote  {"k": "fn", "pg", "y", "x": 0, "t": text of one footnote line}
"pg" is the page number from 1, "y" the distance from the top of the page in points.
The text of a body line is its words joined by single spaces; a word with a footnote
marker is followed by "^N" (N the footnote number); a line broken inside a word at a
soft point ends with "-". The drop-cap letter is not part of the text of the lines.
"g" has one integer for each gap between words of the line: its width in points.

3. GEOMETRY (given)

A page is 36 rows of 12 points. The text measure is 360 points. Widths in points:
  i l I t f j ! . , ; : ' | ( )   4
  m w M W                         12
  - (hyphen)                      5
  other capital letters           10
  every other character           8
  no-break space                  4
A natural space between words is 4. A hyphen added at a soft break is 5. A footnote
marker adds 3 to its word. The first line of a paragraph that follows a paragraph is
indented 24 (not if it is a drop-cap paragraph, and not after a heading). A drop-cap
paragraph has its first three lines indented 30, and the cap is 3 rows high.
Headings (rows): h1 has 3 rows before, is 2 tall, 1 after; h2 has 2 before, 1 tall,
1 after. The space before is dropped at the top of a page.
Footnotes: the lines of a footnote are set at a little under three-quarters of the normal
width of its words (the exact scale and rounding are for you to find), with spaces of 3, to at most 360;
the first line starts with the label "N.". The footnote block of a page is a rule
plus the footnote lines: its height is 18 points plus 9 per line, rounded up to whole
rows, and it sits at the foot of the page; the rule entry has the y of the top of the
block and the footnote lines start 18 points below it, 9 points apart.

4. LINE BREAKING (rules described, constants not given)

Paragraphs are broken into lines by a total-fit method in the manner of TeX's
(Knuth-Plass): the breaks of the whole paragraph are chosen together to minimise a
total of demerits, not line by line. Glue (the spaces) can stretch and shrink; a line's
badness comes from how far its spaces are stretched or shrunk, growing steeply; lines
fall in fitness classes (tight, normal, loose, very loose) and joining lines whose
classes are too different costs extra; a break at a hyphen costs a penalty (a break
at a hard hyphen costs less than one inside a word), two hyphen breaks in a row cost
extra, and so does a hyphen break on the line before the last line; a last line that is
only a very short word costs extra too. The search is made with a tolerance for badness;
if no breaking exists the tolerance is raised, and as a last resort overfull lines are
accepted. All arithmetic is in integers. When two breakings are equally good the
reference picks one in a fixed way. The first line of a drop-cap paragraph, and the
indents above, change the width available to the lines concerned.

Justification: all lines but the last of a paragraph are set to the full measure; the
spare points are shared among the gaps in a fixed way (see the samples). The last line
of a paragraph is not stretched: its gaps are at most the natural space.

Hyphenation: a word may be broken at a hard hyphen (but not within its first two
characters), and plain lower-case words of six letters or more have soft break points
found by a simple vowel and consonant pattern, with some letter pairs that are never
split and a few exception words that have their own points. A word that carries a
footnote marker is not broken at soft points.

5. PAGES (rules described, constants not given)

Lines are placed top to bottom. A page is full when its body rows plus the rows of its
footnote block would not fit in 36 rows. A paragraph can be split between pages, but:
short paragraphs are not split, a split leaves a minimum number of lines on each side,
and a drop-cap paragraph needs its three rows (a drop-cap paragraph of one or two
lines takes three rows). A page does not end on a line that ends in a soft hyphen. A heading is kept with what follows it: if the beginning of
the next block (a few lines of a paragraph, counting the footnotes they bring) does not
fit on the page too, the heading goes to the next page; consecutive headings are kept
together, and set closer than a heading that follows text. A page that has only just been started never refuses what it is given.

Footnotes: all the footnotes of the lines of a page go on that page. When a line's
footnotes do not fit but part of one of them would, the footnote may be split between
pages, with a minimum number of lines on each side; the footnotes after it and the rest
of it are carried to the top of the footnote block of the next page and take room there
before the page's own footnotes. If lines are still carried when the document ends,
they get a page of their own.

References: the page number printed by a reference changes the widths of its line, so
the document is laid out again, starting from the assumption that every anchor is on
page 3 and using each time the pages the anchors fell on, until the anchors stop
moving; the number of layouts is limited (the limit is not given). The page of an anchor is the page where the
first line of its paragraph is placed.
